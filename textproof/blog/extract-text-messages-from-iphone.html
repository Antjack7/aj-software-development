How to Extract Text Messages from an iPhone

13 October 2026 · Anthony Jackson

There are five realistic ways to get messages off an iPhone as something you can read, print or send on. They are not equivalent. One gives you the exact database and needs a computer. One needs nothing but your thumb and produces something nobody wants to read. Here is the honest trade-off for each.

1. Desktop tools that read an iTunes backup

iMazing, Decipher TextMessage, AnyTrans and iExplorer all work the same way underneath. You back the phone up to a Mac or PC, the tool opens the backup's message database, and you export a conversation as PDF, CSV or text.

This is a very complete method, and it is worth saying so plainly. You get every message in the thread, with true timestamps, exactly as the phone stored them — not as they happened to appear on screen. For anything where completeness genuinely matters, this is the method to use.

The costs are real though. You need a computer and a cable, you need enough disk space for a full backup, and if the backup is encrypted you need that password — which is the point where most people stop, because it was set years ago and never written down. Most of these tools are paid, and some renew every year, so check before you buy.

2. Screenshots

Free, instant, and everybody's first instinct.

The trouble is what you end up with: forty images in a camera roll, out of order once they are attached to an email, with no page numbers and no way for a reader to tell whether anything is missing between one shot and the next. If somebody has to read it in sequence — a solicitor, a letting agent, an HR manager — screenshots make that work their problem rather than yours.

Fine for showing a friend one exchange. Poor for anything longer than a screen or two.

3. Apple's own export

Apple does not provide a way to export a Messages conversation as a document. You can forward individual messages, and on a Mac you can print a conversation from the Messages app, but there is no built-in "export this thread" on the iPhone itself.

Data and Privacy at privacy.apple.com lets you request a copy of the data Apple holds about you. It arrives later as files, not as a readable transcript of a conversation, so it answers a different question to the one most people are asking.

4. Importing from Messages with a Shortcut

New in TextProof 1.0.3, on iOS 26.4 or later. You add the free TextProof Shortcut once from Apple's Shortcuts app and turn on its automation. Then you pick a chat and the dates inside TextProof, and the messages come straight in from Apple's Messages app, each with the exact date and time Messages recorded, to the second. Every day in the range is checked and counted, photos sent in the chat are printed in the PDF, and reactions are shown as reactions rather than as typed words.

No computer, no cable, no backup password, nothing uploaded. The limits: it needs iOS 26.4 or later, it covers Apple's Messages app only, and there is a minute of set-up the first time.

5. On-device transcription from a screen recording

The approach that works for any app, and TextProof's other way in. You screen-record yourself scrolling through the conversation, the app reads the recording with the iPhone's own text recognition, and you get a timestamped document as PDF, CSV or plain text.

No computer, no cable, no backup password, nothing uploaded anywhere. Because it reads what was displayed rather than a specific database, it can be used with any app that shows messages on screen. We have tested it most with Messages and WhatsApp, so check the result closely for anything else.

The honest limitation is the mirror of method one. It can only capture what the recording actually passed over. Scroll too fast and content goes by unread; a timestamp that was never on screen cannot be invented. TextProof marks those gaps in the document rather than quietly closing them up, and lets you check and correct every message before exporting — but a gap marked honestly is still a gap.

Which to choose

  • You have the computer, the cable and the backup password, and completeness matters more than convenience — use a desktop backup tool.
  • You need one exchange to show somebody informally — screenshots are fine.
  • The chat is in Messages and your iPhone is on iOS 26.4 or later — import it from Messages. Exact times, and no computer.
  • The messages are in WhatsApp — WhatsApp's own Export Chat, which TextProof can turn straight into a PDF.
  • You need a readable, printable document from any other app, and you only have your phone — on-device transcription.

A note on using any of this formally

Whether a document is accepted in a dispute, a tribunal or a court is decided by the people running that process, not by the app that produced it. What helps in every case is the same: a complete thread rather than a convenient extract, visible dates, and no silent gaps. That is true whichever method above you pick, and it is worth getting right the first time, because re-doing it later looks worse than doing it slowly now.

If the messages are destined for a solicitor or a formal complaint, the longer piece on saving text messages for court goes through what tends to be asked for. If you want the straightforward PDF route, exporting iPhone messages to PDF covers it step by step.
