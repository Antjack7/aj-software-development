Kid Keys

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 5 November 2026

The short version: Kid Keys is a keyboard. It checks what the child types, on the child's phone, inside the keyboard. It never reads what the child receives, and it never sends a message anywhere. The only things that leave the phone are a flagged phrase with its category, a count of keys typed per hour, and, if the family sets it up, the name of an app that was opened and the time. The parent sees categories and counts, never messages. The child sees the same summary the parent sees.

What we collect and why

  • Flagged phrases. When the keyboard notices something risky being typed (a home address, a school, a phone number, pressure to keep a secret, unkind words, words about self-harm), it gives the child a heads-up first. If the child sends it anyway, or removes it, the flagged phrase, its category, the time, and what the child chose are sent to our server. Never the message around it. The parent's phone shows the category, the time, the flagged words, and what the child chose. The child sees exactly the same.
  • Typing counts. How many keys were typed in each hour of the day. Numbers only, never which keys. This shows when the keyboard is in use and whether it has been switched off.
  • App opens (optional). If the family sets up the "See when apps open" automation in Apple's Shortcuts app on the child's phone, it sends us the name of the app and the time it opened. Nothing about what happened inside the app. The child can switch this off at any time, and the parent is told when it goes quiet.
  • Pairing. The child's first name and age, the parent's first name, and a six-digit code, so the two phones can find each other.
  • Alerts. A push notification token for the parent's phone, so we can send an alert when something serious comes up. The alert carries the category only, never the phrase.
  • A device identifier we make up, so the server can tell phones apart. It is not an Apple or Google identifier.

What we do not collect

No messages, sent or received. No passwords: the iPhone switches to its own keyboard in password boxes, and Kid Keys never sees them. No contacts, no location, no browsing history, no photos, no advertising identifiers. The child's own street and school names, if entered, stay on the child's phone and are never uploaded, not even to the parent.

Where the checking happens

All checking happens on the child's phone, inside the keyboard. Nothing is sent to an AI service. With Full Access switched off in iPhone Settings, the keyboard still types but cannot send anything at all.

Open, not secret

The keyboard shows "Kid Keys · on" the whole time it is in use. The child sees exactly the same weekly summary the parent sees, and can see the list of what is collected in the app. The child can switch to another keyboard at any time; the parent then sees "keyboard not used since …" rather than a quiet week.

Keeping and deleting

Flagged phrases are kept for 60 days, typing counts and app opens for 30 days, then deleted automatically. Tapping Start again on the parent's phone deletes the family's details and everything collected from our server. Tapping Unlink on the child's phone frees the code and stops anything further being sent. You can also email the address below with your pairing code.

Where the data is kept

On a Supabase server in London. We do not sell or share data with anyone except the services that make the app work: Supabase (storage) and Expo (push notifications).

Children

Kid Keys is made for children aged 8 to 14, set up by a parent or carer. The parent sets it up first, on their own phone, and the child's phone joins with a code. We collect only what is described above, and the child can always see it.
